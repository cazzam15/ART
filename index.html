Section A — Visual Elements
Q1 · Exam Technique
A student writes: "The painting has dark colours and the figures are small." Will this gain a mark in the SQA exam?
AYes — it correctly identifies two visual elements
BNo — it is purely descriptive and does not justify the effect of these elements
CYes — as long as the statement is correct it will always gain marks
DYes — it mentions two separate points so gets 2 marks
B — No, purely descriptive responses do NOT gain marks. SQA marking principle: marks require JUSTIFIED responses, not description. The student must explain the EFFECT: e.g. "The dark colours create an oppressive, threatening atmosphere, reinforcing the sense of danger in the scene."
Q2 · Multiple Choice
Which of the following responses about COLOUR would gain a mark in the SQA exam?
A"The painting uses blue and green colours."
B"There is a lot of colour in this picture."
C"Cool blue and green tones are used throughout, which create a calm, peaceful atmosphere and suggest the chill of the early morning light."
D"The colours are nice and make the painting look good."
C — this is the only justified response. It: (1) identifies the specific colours, (2) names the type (cool tones), and (3) explains the EFFECT on mood. A, B, and D are all descriptive or vague — they identify colour without explaining its significance or effect.
Q3 · Fill in the Blanks — Visual Elements
Complete the descriptions of key visual elements:
refers to the lightness or darkness of a colour, from highlight to shadow.
is the arrangement of elements within an artwork to create structure and lead the eye.
describes how materials have been applied — e.g. thick impasto or fine detail.
Tone = lightness/darkness. Composition = arrangement of elements. Media handling (or techniques) = how materials have been applied and their effect.
Q4 · Short Answer (4 marks)
Referring to an artist you have studied, identify TWO influences on their work and describe how each influence can be seen in any of their artworks. (4 marks — 1 mark per influence identified, 1 mark per description)
Model answer structure (example using Peploe):
Influence 1: Fauvism / French Post-Impressionism (specific — 1 mark)
How seen: "This influenced Peploe to use bold, non-naturalistic colour in his still life paintings, such as the vibrant reds and oranges in his paintings of roses, which are used for expressive effect rather than to accurately represent reality." (1 mark)

Influence 2: Cézanne\'s approach to structure and form (specific — 1 mark)
How seen: "Peploe adopted Cézanne\'s approach of using colour to build up form, visible in the way he uses planes of colour to suggest the rounded volume of fruit in his still life compositions." (1 mark)

Note: "influenced by French artists" is NOT specific enough — you must name the movement/artist.
Q5 · Multiple Choice
WARM colours in colour theory are associated with which qualities?
ABlues, greens, and purples — associated with calm and cold
BReds, oranges, and yellows — appear to advance, create energy and warmth
CBlacks, whites, and greys — neutral tones
DAll bright colours regardless of hue
B — reds, oranges, and yellows. Warm colours appear to advance in a composition and are associated with warmth, energy, fire, and passion. Cool colours (blues, greens, purples) recede and feel calm or cold. This warm/cool contrast can create depth, mood, and visual interest in an artwork.
SQA Key Areas: Function · Fitness for purpose · Target market · Visual impact · Style · Materials & techniques · Layout · Typography
Section A — Design Issues
Q1 · Multiple Choice
A poster has bold typography, high-contrast colours, and a large striking image. Which design issue does this BEST demonstrate?
AFunction — it works as intended
BVisual impact — it immediately grabs the viewer\'s attention
CFitness for purpose — it fulfils its brief
DMaterials and techniques — it uses specific production methods
B — Visual impact. Visual impact is the immediate, powerful impression a design makes — how strongly it grabs attention. Bold typography, high contrast, and striking imagery all contribute to strong visual impact. Particularly important in advertising and communication design where you have only seconds to attract attention.
Q2 · Short Answer (2 marks)
Explain what is meant by TARGET MARKET in design and explain why it is important. (2 marks)
Model answer:
Target market = the specific group of people the design is aimed at — defined by factors such as age, gender, lifestyle, interests, and income level. (1 mark)
Important because: all design decisions (aesthetics, materials, scale, price, communication approach) must be appropriate for this audience — a design that does not suit its target market will fail commercially. (1 mark)
Q3 · Fill in the Blanks — Design Issues
refers to WHAT the product is designed to do — its intended purpose.
means the design successfully fulfils its function for its intended users in its intended context.
The way text, images and space are organised in a design to guide the viewer is called .
Function = what it does. Fitness for purpose = successfully achieves its function for its users. Layout = organisation of elements to guide the viewer and communicate the message.
Q4 · Multiple Choice
Which design area would you MOST likely discuss when analysing a shopping bag, cereal box, or company logo?
AGraphic/communication design
BArchitectural design
CJewellery design
DInterior design
A — Graphic/communication design. This includes: logos, posters, packaging, advertising, book covers, websites, and signage. Key design issues: visual communication, typography, colour, layout, imagery, and how effectively it communicates to the target audience.

Section A — Art Movements
Q1 · Multiple Choice
The IMPRESSIONIST movement (c.1860s–1880s) was significantly influenced by the invention of collapsible metal paint tubes. Why?
AIt allowed artists to use metallic colours for the first time
BArtists could now paint outdoors (en plein air), directly observing natural light — instead of being tied to the studio
CIt made paint cheaper so more people could become artists
DIt allowed paint to be applied more thickly in impasto technique
B — en plein air painting. Before portable paint tubes, artists mixed paint in pots and had to work in studios. Collapsible metal tubes (invented 1841) let artists take paint outdoors. This technological influence directly enabled Impressionism's core practice of capturing changing natural light directly from observation.
Q2 · Multiple Choice
POP ART (1950s–60s) was influenced by which social and cultural developments?
AMedieval religious iconography and church architecture
BPost-war consumer culture, mass media, advertising, television, and popular culture
CJapanese woodblock printing and Eastern art traditions
DRenaissance techniques and classical Greek sculpture
B — consumer culture and mass media. Pop Art emerged in 1950s Britain and 1960s USA, directly responding to post-war consumerism, mass production, advertising, television, and celebrity culture. Artists like Warhol and Lichtenstein incorporated commercial imagery (soup cans, comic strips, advertising techniques) into fine art.
Q3 · Short Answer (3 marks)
Name THREE different types of influence (from the SQA list) that can affect an artist or designer\'s work, and give a brief example of each.
Model answer (1 mark each, any 3 from SQA list):
• Other artists/art movements: e.g. the Colourists were influenced by Fauvism, leading to their bold use of non-naturalistic colour. (1)
• National/world events: e.g. Picasso created Guernica in response to the bombing of a Basque town during the Spanish Civil War. (1)
• Personal circumstances: e.g. Frida Kahlo\'s physical injuries from a bus accident became the central theme of her self-portraits. (1)
• Technology: e.g. digital tools allow graphic designers to create complex layouts and illustrations more efficiently than by hand. (1)
Q4 · Multiple Choice
The BAUHAUS school (Germany 1919–1933) was influential in design because it:
ARevived historical ornamental decoration
BCombined art, craft, and industrial design under "form follows function" — influencing modern design worldwide
CFounded the Impressionist movement
DIntroduced Pop Art to Germany
B — form follows function. Bauhaus (founded 1919 by Walter Gropius) united fine art, craft, and industrial design. Its principle "form follows function" — design should be determined by purpose, not decoration — profoundly influenced modern graphic design, architecture, typography, and product design worldwide.

🎨 Art & Design Key Reference

Essential vocabulary and knowledge for the SQA exam

🎨 Expressive Visual Elements

Colour
Hue (colour name) · Tone (lightness/darkness) · Saturation (intensity/purity)
Warm colours (red/orange/yellow): advance, energetic
Cool colours (blue/green/purple): recede, calm
Complementary colours: opposite on colour wheel — maximum contrast
Harmonious colours: adjacent on colour wheel — unified effect
Line
Defines edges and outlines · Creates direction and movement
Horizontal = calm/stable · Vertical = strength/height
Diagonal = movement/tension · Curved = flowing/organic
Jagged = energy/aggression · Gestural = spontaneous/expressive
Tone
Range from lightest highlight to darkest shadow
Creates illusion of 3D form (tonal modelling)
Tonal contrast: dramatic (high contrast) or subtle (low contrast)
Chiaroscuro: extreme light/dark contrast (Caravaggio, Rembrandt)
Texture
Actual texture: physically felt on surface (impasto, collage, sculpture)
Implied texture: visual illusion on 2D surface (hatching, stippling, mark-making)
Impasto: thick paint application — creates raised, tactile surface
Describes HOW the material has been applied
Shape & Form
Shape: 2D, flat (circle, triangle, organic shape)
Form: 3D, has volume and mass (sphere, cube, organic form)
Geometric: mathematical, precise · Organic: natural, irregular
Form created in 2D through tonal modelling and perspective
Pattern
Repetition of visual elements (shape, line, colour, texture)
Regular: geometric, predictable · Irregular: varied, organic
Creates rhythm, visual interest, cultural identity
Central to textile design, decorative arts, and many painting traditions
Composition, Scale & Other Elements
Composition: arrangement of elements — focal point, balance, perspective, viewpoint
Scale: relative size — creates emphasis, depth, visual hierarchy
Style: distinctive visual characteristics of an artist or movement
Mood & Atmosphere: emotional quality — HOW was it achieved?
Visual Impact: how powerfully it grabs the viewer\'s attention

✏️ Design Elements & Issues

Function & Fitness for Purpose
Function: what the product/design is made to DO
Fitness for purpose: does it successfully achieve its function for its intended users?
Must consider: safety, durability, ease of use, appropriateness for target market
Target Market
The specific group the design is aimed at
Influences: aesthetics, materials, price, scale, communication approach
Research into: age, lifestyle, income, interests, needs
All design decisions should be tailored to the target market
Layout & Typography
Layout: organisation of text, images, space to guide the viewer
Visual hierarchy: most → least important; guides reading order
Typography: typeface choice, size, weight, spacing, colour
Serif = traditional · Sans-serif = modern · Script = elegant/personal
Design Areas (Question Paper)
Graphics/communication design: logos, posters, packaging, advertising
Product design: furniture, appliances, tools, electronics
Interior/environmental/architectural design: spaces and buildings
Jewellery design: wearable decorative objects
Fashion/textiles: clothing, fabric, pattern design

🌍 Key Influences on Artists & Designers

SQA List of Influences
Living conditions (economic) · Family and community · Other artists/art movements
Exposure to different cultures · Physical environment/geography
New and emerging technology · Politics · Gender
Religion/belief · National/world events
Other fields (science, literature) · Personal circumstances · Health and wellbeing
How to Answer Influences Questions
IDENTIFY the specific influence (1 mark): e.g. "Peploe was influenced by the Fauves"
DESCRIBE how it can be seen in the work (1 mark): e.g. "which led to his use of bold, non-naturalistic colour in his still life paintings"
= 2 marks per influence, 4 marks total (2 influences required)
Key Art Movements
Impressionism: capturing light, loose brushwork, en plein air (1860s–80s)
Post-Impressionism: personal expression, structure, bold colour (1880s–90s)
Fauvism: bold non-naturalistic colour for expression (c.1905)
Expressionism: emotional distortion and intense colour (1905–30s)
Cubism: multiple viewpoints simultaneously (c.1908)
Art Nouveau: organic forms, flowing lines, craft (1890–1910)
Bauhaus: function + craft + industrial design (1919–33)
Pop Art: consumer culture, mass media imagery (1950s–60s)
Scottish Artists to Know
Scottish Colourists: Peploe, Cadell, Hunter, Fergusson
Influenced by: Fauvism, Post-Impressionism, French avant-garde
Known for: vibrant colour, painterly technique, still life, landscapes, figures
Charles Rennie Mackintosh: Art Nouveau architect/designer, Glasgow Style

📋 Exam Technique & Portfolio

Question Paper Structure
Section 1: Expressive Art Studies — 20 marks
Q1 (mandatory, 10 marks): your studied artist — 3 prompts for part (a) + influences for part (b)
Q2–6 (optional, pick ONE, 10 marks): unseen artwork
Section 2: Design Studies — 20 marks
Q7 (mandatory): your studied designer
Q8–12 (optional, pick ONE): unseen design
How to Write Justified Answers
DESCRIBE the visual element → EXPLAIN its EFFECT
❌ "The painting has bright colours." (0 marks — description only)
✅ "The artist used warm, vibrant reds and oranges which contrast with the cool blues, creating a sense of depth and drawing the viewer\'s eye to the focal point." (mark awarded)
Always ask: WHY? WHAT EFFECT does it have?
Portfolio Requirements
Both portfolios max 3 A2-sized single-sided sheets (must fold to A1)
Expressive portfolio: theme → research → line of development → final piece → evaluation
Design portfolio: brief → research + market research → line of development → solution → evaluation
Evaluation: mandatory SQA template — justified personal opinions
